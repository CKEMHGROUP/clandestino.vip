Our story

A generation that left

From the 1960s into the early 1970s, well over a million Portuguese left a country governed by the Estado Novo. They went to France, Germany, Switzerland, Luxembourg and beyond, for wages the villages could not pay and, for many young men, to avoid the colonial wars.

Ours was one of those families: our relatives left under that regime. We do not tell it as a story of hardship alone. We tell it because it belongs to thousands of families like ours, and because what comes next is still unwritten.

The elders, and the traditions that travelled with them. Family archive.

Industrial Europe: the mill floors that employed a generation of emigrants.

Work came first

In foreign mills and factories the first generation did the work its host countries needed. The hours were long, the languages were new, and most of what was earned was sent home or saved.

It built a reputation for reliability and almost no capital.

The generation that grew up between two countries. Family archive.

Between two countries

Their children grew up between two languages and worked as hard as their parents. Few were taught how capital is built, protected and passed on, because nobody had taught their parents.

That gap is what this project sets out to close.

Lineage

Three origins, one lineage

Andrade, Azevedo and Costa are not three households. They are three origins, each with its own village, trade and history, joined over time through marriage. The present generation is the product of all three.

Why this exists

I am the product of all three origins. They taught me that wealth without character does not last, and that character without education does not build much. This project is how I intend to pass on both.
